Stadium Elevation & Batted-Ball Flight

How Does Elevation Shape Baseball Flight?

While our homepage shows that home run rates climb with ballpark altitude, MLB Statcast ball-tracking data lets us test why: do baseballs actually travel farther in higher-altitude stadiums, or are hitters simply hitting the ball harder? Below, we examine 62,145 tracked fly balls and line drives from the 2024 MLB regular season across all 30 ballparks, comparing stadium elevation against average batted-ball distance (bbdist), exit velocity (launch_speed), and launch angle (launch_angle).

What Visitors Are Seeing

Average airborne batted-ball distance rises noticeably with stadium elevation—peaking at Coors Field (5,180 ft), with other higher-elevation ballparks like Chase Field (1,086 ft), Kauffman Stadium (910 ft), and Target Field (840 ft) also ranking near the top. Crucially, the comparison table shows that average exit velocity (~92–94 mph) and launch angle (~26.5°–29°) vary very little from park to park, pointing to thinner air and reduced aerodynamic drag—not harder swings—as a key driver of extra carry at altitude.
